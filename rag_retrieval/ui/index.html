Hybrid RAG retrieval

Permission-aware BM25 + dense retrieval, RRF fusion and cross-encoder reranking, compared with vector-only search

The pipeline

Permissions are applied first, inside both indexes, so nothing the reader can't see is ranked, reranked or sent to the model.

Why hybrid

  • Dense retrieval matches meaning: "I can't get into the corporate network from home" finds the VPN guide without sharing a word with it.
  • It blurs identifiers. INC-2291, INC-2287 and INC-2302 embed almost identically, as do PAY-4031 and BILL-4031. BM25 matches the exact token.
  • RRF merges the two rankings by rank, so cosine similarities and BM25 scores never need to be put on one scale.
  • The cross-encoder reads query and chunk together and fixes the order, which matters most for the top 1–3 results an LLM actually uses.

Three ways to enforce permissions

  • Pre-filter (used here): the permission mask is applied inside BM25 and the vector search, so the top k is the best k the reader may see. No leaks, full result lists.
  • Post-filter: rank over everything, then drop forbidden results. No leaks, but users get fewer than k results, and the gaps are larger for less-privileged users.
  • None: what an unprotected index does. Restricted salary bands, layoff plans and unannounced results show up for anyone who asks.
  • Pre-filter also reports how many forbidden documents would have ranked, as a count only. Showing their titles would itself be a leak.

Access rule

Each document carries a classification (public internal confidential restricted) and a list of groups. A reader can see it when they belong to one of the groups and their clearance is at least the classification. The ACL is copied onto every chunk at index time, so filtering needs no join at query time.
